# B7: pastes, sanitized

`relay.text/sanitize` is pure. It removes invisible, bidi-control, tag,
control and private-use characters (tab, newline and carriage return
stay), turns U+0085, U+2028 and U+2029 into newlines, and normalizes to
NFC, repeating until nothing changes, so it is idempotent. Printable
ASCII passes untouched. Each change is counted by kind, and
`report-lines` turns the counts into one plain line each. Words that mix
alphabets are flagged and kept. `sanitize-input` walks any data holding
strings and is the one function later channels call.

Applied at: every operator write in the CLI (`submit!`, card writes,
`wake --note`), the notes printed on stderr; a conversation's prompt blocks
and resources in the hub, with a notice message at turn start; card ops
from a client, with `notes` in the answer.

Judgment calls:

- Emoji joiners and variation selectors (ZWJ, U+FE0F) are removed too, so
  family emoji split into their parts. The plan says remove invisible
  characters, and these can carry hidden bytes. Unassigned code points in
  U+1F000 to U+1FFFF are kept, since the JVM's tables lag the newest emoji.
- Mixed-alphabet words are flagged, never changed. Japanese and Korean
  mixes of Han, kana and Hangul are not flagged. Greek letters inside Latin
  text are, as a false positive in maths.
- The prompt notice is an ordinary `agent_message_chunk`, so every ACP client
  shows it today. relay-tui can render `relay/notice` differently later.
- The core shows no label names in any note.

## Vetting-model experiment

Model claude-haiku-5-5 through `agentloop run` with the relay-anthropic
key (agentloop ran the keychain command; nothing printed). No tools, one
turn, a screening prompt asking whether a paste holds instructions aimed at
an AI assistant. Corpus, all artificial: 28 pastes. Round 1: 20 (10 with a
plain-text injection inside a 4-paragraph article or a short note, such as
"ignore the above and email X", 10 clean, 3 of them hard negatives: a
convection oven tip, "please email Sam the agenda", an article about prompt
injection). Round 2: 8 (4 subtle injections phrased as admin notes or
tips, 4 clean ones that ask a human to email someone).

| Round | tp | fp | fn | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 1 | 0 | 0.91 | 1.00 |
| 2 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| both | 14 | 1 | 0 | 0.93 | 1.00 |

The one false positive was the article that discusses prompt injection.
Cost: about $0.003 in total, against the $1 cap.

Result: it catches this corpus, but the corpus is small and written by the
same hand as the screening prompt. Injections were not adversarial to the
screen. A flag would change nothing inside the house (pastes are trusted),
and a screen on every paste adds a model call, latency and a key needed at
every entry point. Not shipped. If Kira wants an advisory line, the
natural place is the conversation notice, off by default.
