# B8: the mailbox and replies

Ingestors bring what arrives from outside into relay as memos to a role.
They are code with no model, configured in relay.edn's `:ingest` and
polled by the tick. There are three kinds:

- `:pop3`: the agent's mailbox, read with curl.
- `:github`: issues and pull requests on the repositories Kira lists,
  through the public REST API.
- `:ics`: a calendar feed by URL, read only.

`relay.ingest` holds the pure parts: settings, schedule, labels, memo
text and what is new. `relay.mailbox` parses mail and gives the
verdict. `relay.ics-feed` reads feeds. `relay.ingest-run` does the
fetching. `relay.core` records a poll as `:ingest/record` or
`:ingest/failed`, with new events `ingest/polled`, `ingest/failed` and
`message/replied`. `relay poll [<source>]` checks now.

What an ingestor brings in is labelled `contaminated` plus a source
label in agentloop's label charset: `mail:<sender>`,
`github:<owner>/<repo>` or `ics:<source>`. relay used to refuse every
label but `private` and `contaminated`. It now also accepts a source
label (`relay.core/known-label?`: a known name, or `kind:what` in the
A1 charset) on commands, on the labels a harness sends with a call, and
on a wake's end. Tool output and relay.edn tool `:labels` are still
limited to the two names. Kira sees the source in words: the outside
marker becomes `[from outside: a mail that says it is from
eve@example[.]net]` when exactly one source label applies, and `[from
outside]` otherwise. The From header is unauthenticated, so the address
is shown as a claim and the word "outside" stays (fix round 1). A mail
that names one of Kira's addresses and fails verification is labelled
`mail:unverified-operator`, never with her address, and is shown as "a
mail that says it is from you, not verified"; its memo subject is
"Unverified mail in the operator's name: ...".

A mail is Kira's, and trusted (`:operator-mail`, I5), only when all of
these hold: it has one From header naming one address, which is the
`:to` of an email channel; every header line ends CRLF and parses;
exactly one `Authentication-Results` header carries the source's
`:authserv-id`, and every one of them parses; and that header says
`dkim=pass` with `header.d` her address's domain. Comments, folding,
quoted values and case are handled. A comment saying `dkim=pass` does
not count. Two properties cover this. One checks that the verdict
equals an oracle over generated headers, including forged and duplicate
`Authentication-Results`, folding, decoy comments and bare CR or LF. The
other checks that the core never records an unverified mail as from the
operator or as a reply.

A verified mail whose `In-Reply-To` or `References` names a Message-ID
relay made becomes a `message/replied` event on that message. The ID
has to be at an agent domain, name a message that exists, and carry
that message's creation time. The mail also becomes a memo from
`operator` to the role that sent the message, with the quoted tail
removed. `_relay/messages` shows replies. Other verified mail reaches the
source's role as Kira's own.

Judgment calls, conservative where the brief was silent:

- relay never deletes from the mailbox. It remembers UIDLs, reads at
  most 20 new mails a poll, and reads a mail over 10 MB as headers
  only. Each curl call is its own POP3 session, so relay reads UIDL
  again after fetching and drops the batch if the numbering changed.
  Attachments are listed by name, type and size, not kept.
- IMAP is not done. curl could, but mailpit has no IMAP to test against.
- A reply that is not verified gets no reply event and does not join
  Kira's thread. It is mailbox content to the source's role, and the memo
  says what it claims to answer and that it could not be confirmed.
- Exactly one `Authentication-Results` header from the configured
  authserv-id is required. Two make the mail unverified, because a
  provider that does not strip forged copies cannot be told apart.
  Without `:authserv-id` no mail is trusted. `header.d` must equal her
  domain exactly; a subdomain does not count.
- The first GitHub poll of a repository only records where to start, so
  an old backlog does not arrive as news. Each repository's new and
  updated items (a changed `updated_at`) go in memos of at most 50, and
  every item a poll returns is recorded at once (fix round 1). Capping
  them would lose items for good, since the stored `since` cursor is
  past all of them. The
  ETag is kept only while `since` stays the same, since another
  `since` is another query.
- A calendar memo lists new, changed and removed events that are
  upcoming (ended less than a day ago, or repeating with no end yet).
  Events that are over drop out quietly. The version digest carries the
  title, so a removal can still say what was removed. At most 50 new or
  changed and 50 removed events a poll; the rest come next poll, which
  fetches the whole feed again. A 200 whose body does not start with
  `BEGIN:VCALENDAR` and hold `END:VCALENDAR` is a failed poll (fix round
  1), so an error page never reads as every event removed. A whole
  calendar with no events still removes them all: that is what an
  emptied calendar looks like.
- Polling is stateless: a source is due at minutes whose number is a
  multiple of `:every-minutes` (defaults 5, 15 and 60). A missed tick
  skips one poll, and nothing is written to the log for a quiet poll.
- No redirect is followed. Plain `pop3://` and `http://` are taken only
  to this machine. No error quotes a calendar URL, since it may hold a
  token.
- A failing source is recorded when it starts failing or fails another
  way, and Kira gets one alert after `:outage-patience-minutes`. A
  successful poll clears it.
- `:to` must be a role that is not public. While that role is not
  active, the source is not polled, and nothing is lost.
