# B11: what's ready

The client channel now has a view of everything prepared for Kira, not
only of messages. `relay.ready` is a pure fold over the state that gives
one list, newest first. Each item has a kind, a title, lines, and where it
came from in plain words (`relay.ready/provenance`: "from a mail by x@y",
"from the web", "from outside", nothing for her own things, never a label's
name). The kinds are message, draft (what an edit-class tool call
prepared), event (the agent's calendar, until it ends) and finding (an
active card a role put on the board). `relay.ready/sources` is the list of
source functions, so B9's file moves are one more entry there and a word in
the client's kind table.

Drafts are recorded. After a wake, and as a conversation goes,
`relay.dispatch/store-prepared!` reads the session's entries
(`agentloop/parse-calls`) and, for each allowed call that ended without an
error to a tool relay.edn gives class `edit`, submits `:prepared/record`
(event `prepared/recorded`) with the session's labels at the call. The
title is the input's subject, title, name or summary; where it went is the
first line of the result; the preview is the input's body or text. A call
is recorded once.

Surfaces: `relay ready` (60 columns, headings at the left edge, every line
of other people's text indented), `_relay/ready` with
`_relay/ready/changed` (new or different items by id, plus the ids gone;
the hub keeps what each connection holds, as it does for the board), and
`_relay/reply`. In relay-tui, `R` opens a full-screen Ready view.

Judgment calls, conservative where the plan was silent:

- Nothing leaves the view except by time or by the thing itself changing:
  an event that ended, a card archived, an item past the newest 60. There
  is no dismissing yet; B10 remembers dismissed suggestions, and
  dismissing here would be a second mechanism for the same thing.
- A reply is `:message/reply`, recorded as `reply/received` against the
  message plus a memo from the operator to the role that told her. A
  message relay wrote itself has nobody to answer and is refused. This is
  the client channel's half; B8 turns replies on every channel into the
  same event, and the memo is how the role hears of one meanwhile.
- The reply memo carries the labels of the message subject it quotes. The
  reply text is the operator's own and carries none.
- Labels beyond `contaminated` and `private` (B8's `mail:x@y`) are not
  accepted by the core yet, so the mapping from them to words is tested on
  the pure function and the wire only. Any label other than `private`
  reads as "from outside" and makes text defanged.
- The reply input in relay-tui takes no paste, like a card's editor, so a
  pasted reply cannot carry hidden text in before B7's sanitizing lands.
- The ready view is hub-mode only. Against a plain ACP agent `R` does
  nothing.
- Drafts show their tool's first result line as "where". For a tool that
  does not say where, that line is whatever it said; a per-tool "where"
  template is a later refinement.

Tests: `ready_test` (fold, newest first, bounded, provenance properties,
wire, changes applied by a client equal asking again, prepared recording
once, replies, `relay ready`, the hub's `_relay/ready` and `_relay/reply`,
a session's calls through the shell). relay-tui: wire, core (an invariants
property over random runs), render (goldens at 59 and 119 columns, a fit
property, no label names), ui keys, fakehub and a pty test of `R`, reply,
and the answer arriving.

## After the rebase onto B7, B8 and B9

Replies take B8's path. `:message/reply` (the client channel and `relay
reply`) and a verified mail reply both go through
`relay.core/record-reply`: a memo from the operator to the role that told
her (`relay.messages/reply-memo`, the same wording for both, naming the
message and `in_reply_to`), then `message/replied` with the channel's
name. B11's `reply/received` event is gone; it never reached a live log.
The hub cleans a reply with `relay.text/sanitize-input` and returns the
lines in the answer's `notes`; the CLI cleans it as every command does.

- The reply memo carries the labels of the message whose subject it
  quotes, on both channels. B8 gave the mail reply no labels, which would
  launder a subject a role wrote after reading outside text.
- `reply-role` now asks `message-author`, so a mail reply to a message
  that was a memo to the operator reaches the memo's sender, as the
  client's does, instead of the source's role.
- When the message came from a memo to the operator that is still open,
  the reply closes it, as the operator, with the outcome "Answered: "
  and the start of her words. A second reply closes nothing.

Ties in the view are broken by the log's seq (newest first), then id.
Each item carries `seq`, and the wire sends it.

B9's moves are a `move` kind (`relay.ready/sources` gained one entry).
The item names the file when one thing moved, else how many; its lines
are "By <role>." and one "from → to" per move, marked "(undone)" when
put back; `from` is the provenance of the call's labels, the session's.
Undo is offered while a done step is left, and for a call relay never
finished, where undo lists the moves to check by hand. `relay ready`
shows `Undo: relay undo <n>`; `_relay/undo` runs `relay.moves/undo!`
through a new hub effect, `:undo`, whose result comes back like a
submit's, and refuses with B9's reason joined into one line. The
speculative "move" heading in `relay.render/ready-kind` now has items,
so it stays.

`relay ready` shows the 10 newest items and a last line saying how many
older ones `--all` would add. Each heading ends with a short age ("just
now", "5m ago", "3h ago", "2d ago", then the date). A body cut at its
line limit ends with " …". A move may show 12 lines rather than 4,
since its full paths are the point of it.
